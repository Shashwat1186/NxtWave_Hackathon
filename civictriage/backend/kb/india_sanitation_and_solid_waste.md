# Pan-India Solid Waste Management & Public Sanitation — Statutory Jurisdiction Knowledge Base

## National Authority Architecture (All India)
Urban cleanliness, municipal solid waste management, scientific processing, and public sanitation across all Indian States and Union Territories are governed by:
1. **Urban Local Bodies (ULBs)**: Municipal Corporations, Municipal Councils, Nagar Panchayats, and Cantonment Boards acting under the direct statutory framework of the Union Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change (MoEFCC).
2. **State Urban Development & Swachh Bharat Mission (Urban) Directorates**: Executive bodies supervising municipal solid waste infrastructure, biomining, legacy waste landfills, and scientific waste processing plants.

## Scope of Grievances Handled
- Overflowing community garbage bins, dumper placers, secondary collection depots, and garbage vulnerability points (GVPs)
- Uncollected door-to-door residential and commercial municipal solid waste
- Illegal open burning of leaves, municipal solid waste, plastic, and tyres on roadsides and vacant plots
- Construction and Demolition (C&D) waste dumped illegally on footpaths, roadsides, stormwater drains, and lake beds
- Dead animal carcasses (cows, dogs, livestock) left unattended on public streets causing severe biohazard
- Public urination/defecation spots, unhygienic public/community toilets, and unmaintained mobile bio-toilets
- Commercial food waste from hotels/markets dumped untreated into stormwater nalas and roadside gutters

## All-India Statutory Acts, Legal Codes & Technical Standards
- **Solid Waste Management Rules 2016 (SWM Rules 2016, notified under Environment Protection Act 1986)**:
  - *Rule 15*: Direct mandatory duty on local authorities to arrange for daily door-to-door collection, segregated transport, processing of wet/dry/hazardous waste, and elimination of open dumping.
  - *Rule 19*: Strict ban on throwing, burning, or burying solid waste on streets, open public spaces, water bodies, or vacant plots. Mandates on-the-spot spot fines for littering and open burning.
- **Environment (Protection) Act 1986, Sections 5 & 15**: Grants binding statutory power to municipal commissioners and environment officers to seize vehicles, close non-compliant commercial units, and prosecute offenders.
- **Construction and Demolition (C&D) Waste Management Rules 2016**: Mandates separate collection and designated municipal recycling depots for building debris; prohibits dumping on roads or stormwater drains.
- **National Green Tribunal (NGT) Principal Bench Directives**: Strict financial penalties levied against municipal commissioners and urban local bodies for uncollected garbage and open landfill fires (*Almitra H. Patel & Anr. v. Union of India*).
- **Swachh Bharat Mission (Urban) 2.0 Directives**: Star rating protocols for Garbage Free Cities (GFC) and Open Defecation Free (ODF++) service benchmarks.
- **State Municipal Acts (Pan-India)**:
  - *Delhi*: DMC Act 1957 Section 353 (Duty of Commissioner in respect of disposal of rubbish).
  - *Maharashtra*: MMC Act 1888 Section 365 (Scavenging and cleansing of streets).
  - *Telangana*: Telangana Municipalities Act 2019 Section 154 (Duty to clean streets and collect waste).
  - *West Bengal*: KMC Act 1980 Section 312.
  - *Tamil Nadu*: Tamil Nadu Urban Local Bodies Act 1998 Section 128.

## SLA Commitments & Escalation Matrix
- **Critical (Dead animal carcass on street, burning garbage emitting toxic fumes near schools/hospitals, hazardous waste spill)**: **4 Hours**.
- **High (Overflowing municipal dumper placer blocking road or pedestrian movement)**: **12 Hours**.
- **Medium (Missed daily door-to-door collection route, dirty community toilet)**: **24 Hours**.
- **Low (Sanction of secondary bin, sweeping frequency adjustment)**: **72 Hours**.
- **Escalation**: Unresolved complaints escalate to Sanitary Inspector -> Chief Medical Officer of Health (CMOH) / Additional Commissioner (Sanitation) -> Municipal Commissioner -> State Pollution Control Board.
