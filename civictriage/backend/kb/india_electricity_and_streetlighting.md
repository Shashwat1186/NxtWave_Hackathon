# Pan-India Electricity Distribution & Streetlighting — Statutory Jurisdiction Knowledge Base

## National Authority Architecture (All India)
Public road illumination, electrical distribution safety, and low-tension utility assets across all States and Union Territories of India are partitioned into:
1. **State Power Distribution Companies (DISCOMs)**: Public and private power distribution licensees operating under state electricity regulatory commissions (SERCs) — e.g., BESCOM (Karnataka), MSEDCL / Adani / Tata (Maharashtra), TANGEDCO (Tamil Nadu), UPPCL (Uttar Pradesh), BSES / TPDDL (Delhi), WBSEDCL (West Bengal), TSSPDCL / TSNPDCL (Telangana), PSPCL (Punjab), APDCL (Assam). Responsible for poles, conductors, transformers, and electrical supply.
2. **Municipal Corporation Electrical & Public Lighting Wings**: Responsible for luminaires, CCMS automated streetlight control panels, LED fixtures, timer switches, and high-mast decorative lights on city roads under Energy Efficiency Services Limited (EESL) national agreements.

## Scope of Grievances Handled
- Dark road stretches, unlit streets, and broken LED luminaires creating pedestrian safety hazards
- High-mast lighting failure at busy city intersections, traffic roundabouts, and flyover approaches
- Snapped, fallen, or sagging overhead 230V/415V low-tension or 11kV high-tension power cables lying on public streets
- Leaning, damaged, or vehicular-impacted electrical poles at risk of collapsing onto vehicles or pedestrians
- Sparking distribution transformer (DTR) centers, open feeder pillar boxes, exposed underground cable joints, and missing grounding wire
- Electrical shocks or stray voltage reported on municipal poles, handrails, or waterlogged monsoon streets

## All-India Statutory Acts, Legal Codes & Technical Standards
- **The Electricity Act 2003 (Union Legislation — Applicable to All India)**:
  - *Section 53*: Direct statutory mandate requiring all distribution licensees to comply with safety regulations to protect the public from personal injury and fire hazards.
  - *Section 68*: Rules governing overhead lines; mandatory clearances from roads, buildings, and ground levels.
  - *Section 161*: Mandatory reporting of electrical accidents, investigation, and immediate penal action for negligence.
- **Central Electricity Authority (Measures relating to Safety and Electric Supply) Regulations 2010**:
  - *Regulation 29*: Mandatory periodic inspection, earthing, and insulation testing of overhead lines and cables.
  - *Regulation 36*: Earth leakage circuit breaker (ELCB) / Residual Current Device (RCD) mandate for public installations.
  - *Regulation 73*: Mandatory minimum vertical clearance of conductors passing along or across any public street (minimum 5.8m for high-voltage lines, 5.5m for low-voltage lines).
- **National Lighting Code of India (Bureau of Indian Standards SP 72:2010)**: Specifications for public roadway illuminance levels (M1 to M5 categories) to ensure accident reduction and crime prevention.
- **Ministry of Housing & Urban Affairs (MoHUA) Safe City Standards**: Mandatory uninterrupted illumination along public transport hubs, bus shelters, and designated women safety transit corridors.

## SLA Commitments & Escalation Matrix
- **Critical (Snapped live overhead conductor, pole collapse hazard, sparking transformer, electrified pole on flooded road)**: **2 Hours** (Emergency feeder tripping, line disconnection, and site cordoning).
- **High (Multiple consecutive non-functional streetlights on arterial/transit corridor or women transit path)**: **24 Hours**.
- **Medium (Single isolated LED streetlight fixture fault in residential layout)**: **48 Hours**.
- **Low (Aesthetic pole realignment, cable trench backfilling)**: **120 Hours (5 Days)**.
- **Escalation**: Unresolved complaints escalate to Assistant Engineer (Electrical) -> Divisional Engineer -> Superintending Engineer (DISCOM) -> State Electrical Inspectorate.

## Authority Directory Routing
- Primary Authority: State Electricity Distribution Company (DISCOM) & Municipal Electrical Wing
- Pan-India Power Consumer Helpline: **1912** (Universal toll-free emergency electricity complaint number active across all Indian states).
