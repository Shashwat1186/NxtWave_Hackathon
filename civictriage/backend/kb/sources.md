## 1. Constitutional Foundation

The overarching framework for urban governance, civic rights, and administrative jurisdiction in India derives from:

* **Constitution of India — 74th Amendment Act, 1992 (Part IX-A & Twelfth Schedule)**:
  * *Article 243W*: Powers, authority, and responsibilities of Municipalities.
  * *Twelfth Schedule (Articles 243W)*: Specifically Entry 1 (Urban planning), Entry 2 (Regulation of land use), Entry 4 (Roads and bridges), Entry 5 (Water supply for domestic, industrial and commercial purposes), Entry 6 (Public health, sanitation, conservancy and solid waste management), Entry 8 (Urban forestry, protection of the environment and promotion of ecological aspects), and Entry 17 (Public amenities including street lighting, parking lots, bus stops and public conveniences).
* **Constitution of India — Article 21 (Right to Life & Personal Liberty)**:
  * Expanded by the Supreme Court of India to include the right to safe, motorable, and hazard-free public roads (*Common Cause v. Union of India*), the right to clean drinking water (*Subhash Kumar v. State of Bihar*, 1991), and freedom from public nuisance (*Municipal Council, Ratlam v. Shri Vardichand & Ors.*, 1980 AIR 1622).
* **Constitution of India — Articles 48A & 51A(g)**:
  * Constitutional mandate on the State and fundamental duty of every citizen to protect and improve the natural environment, lakes, rivers, forests, and green cover.

## 2. Roads, Highways & Pedestrian Infrastructure

Referenced in: [`backend/kb/india_roads_and_highways.md`](./india_roads_and_highways.md) and [`backend/kb/ghmc_roads.md`](./ghmc_roads.md)

### Central Acts & Parliamentary Legislation
1. **Motor Vehicles Act, 1988 (as amended by the Motor Vehicles (Amendment) Act, 2019 — Act No. 32 of 2019)**:
   * **Section 198A**: *Failure to comply with standards for road design, construction, and maintenance.* Imposes direct statutory liability and penal fines (up to ₹1,00,000) on designated highway/road authorities, engineering consultants, and contractors for failure to adhere to maintenance standards resulting in fatal accidents or physical harm.
2. **National Highways Act, 1956 (Act No. 48 of 1956)**:
   * Establishes Central Government and National Highways Authority of India (NHAI) ownership and maintenance mandates over all declared National Highway corridors.
3. **National Highways Authority of India Act, 1988 (Act No. 68 of 1988)**:
   * Operational guidelines for NHAI Project Implementation Units (PIUs).
4. **Control of National Highways (Land and Traffic) Act, 2002 (Act No. 13 of 2003)**:
   * Prevention of unauthorized occupation and summary removal of encroachments on highway right-of-ways.

### Technical Codes & Standards (Indian Roads Congress - IRC)
* **IRC:82-2015**: *Code of Practice for Maintenance of Bituminous Surfaces of Highways* (Standard procedures for pothole patching, edge repairs, crack sealing, and resurfacing).
* **IRC:SP:72-2015**: *Guidelines for the Design of Flexible Pavements for Low Volume Rural and Urban Colony Roads*.
* **IRC:103-2012**: *Guidelines for Pedestrian Facilities* (Footpath widths, kerb heights, and disabled-friendly tactile paving).
* **MoRTH Specifications for Road and Bridge Works (5th Revision)**: Ministry of Road Transport and Highways national engineering specifications.

### State Municipal Codes (Comparative Pan-India Selection)
* Greater Hyderabad Municipal Corporation Act, 1955 (Section 112: Duty to maintain public streets).
* Mumbai Municipal Corporation Act, 1888 (Section 61(m): Repair and cleansing of public streets).
* Karnataka Municipal Corporations Act, 1976 / BBMP Act, 2020 (Sections on public streets and right-of-way management).
* Delhi Municipal Corporation Act, 1957 (Section 42 & 298: Maintenance of public streets).
* Chennai City Municipal Corporation Act, 1919 (Section 203: Maintenance of public roads).

## 3. Water Supply, Sewerage & Public Health Engineering

Referenced in: [`backend/kb/india_water_and_sewerage.md`](./india_water_and_sewerage.md) and [`backend/kb/hmwssb_water.md`](./hmwssb_water.md)

### Central Acts, Regulatory Frameworks & Supreme Court Directives
1. **Water (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act, 1974 (Act No. 6 of 1974)**:
   * *Section 24*: Prohibition on use of stream or well for disposal of polluting matter.
   * *Section 25*: Restrictions on new outlets and discharges.
   * *Section 33A*: Power of statutory boards to issue directions for immediate stoppage of electricity/water and closure of defaulting entities.
2. **The Prohibition of Employment as Manual Scavengers and their Rehabilitation Act, 2013 (Act No. 25 of 2013)**:
   * Supreme Court of India in *Safai Karamchari Andolan v. Union of India (2014)*: Absolute prohibition on human entry into sewers and septic tanks; mandates mechanized jetting, suction rovers, and automated municipal sewer cleaning.

### National Technical Guidelines & Quality Standards
* **Bureau of Indian Standards — BIS IS 10500:2012**:
  * *Drinking Water — Specification (Second Revision)*: Mandatory chemical, heavy metal, and bacteriological standards for potable municipal water.
* **CPHEEO (Central Public Health & Environmental Engineering Organisation), MoHUA**:
  * *Manual on Water Supply and Treatment (3rd Edition)*.
  * *Manual on Sewerage and Sewage Treatment Systems (Part A: Engineering; Part B: O&M; Part C: Management)*.
* **AMRUT 2.0 (Atal Mission for Rejuvenation and Urban Transformation)**:
  * National Service Level Benchmarks (SLBs): Universal tap water supply (135 lpcd in urban areas) and 100% sewerage network coverage.
* **Jal Jeevan Mission (Urban)**: Ministry of Jal Shakti guidelines on drinking water security.

### Statutory Water Board Charters
* Hyderabad Metropolitan Water Supply and Sewerage Act, 1989 (Sections 12 & 42).
* Delhi Water Board Act, 1998 (Delhi Jal Board mandate).
* Bangalore Water Supply and Sewerage Act, 1964 (BWSSB statutory rules).
* Chennai Metropolitan Water Supply and Sewerage Act, 1978 (CMWSSB).

## 4. Solid Waste Management & Urban Sanitation

Referenced in: [`backend/kb/india_sanitation_and_solid_waste.md`](./india_sanitation_and_solid_waste.md) and [`backend/kb/ghmc_sanitation.md`](./ghmc_sanitation.md)

### Statutory Rules Notified under the Environment (Protection) Act, 1986
1. **Solid Waste Management Rules, 2016 (SWM Rules 2016)**:
   * Notified by Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change (MoEFCC) via Gazette Notification S.O. 1357(E) dated 8 April 2016.
   * *Rule 15*: Duties of local authorities and urban local bodies for daily segregated collection, transport, storage, and scientific disposal.
   * *Rule 19*: Direct statutory prohibition on throwing, burying, or burning solid waste on streets, vacant plots, and water bodies.
   * *Rule 20*: Responsibilities of State Pollution Control Boards.
2. **Construction and Demolition Waste Management Rules, 2016 (Gazette G.S.R. 317(E))**:
   * Segregation, designated municipal collection centers, and prohibition of building debris dumping along public right-of-ways or drains.
3. **Bio-Medical Waste Management Rules, 2016 (Gazette G.S.R. 343(E))**:
   * Strict procedures for infectious waste, clinical disposals, and hazardous medical cleanup.
4. **Plastic Waste Management Rules, 2016 (as amended 2021 & 2022)**:
   * Ban on single-use plastics and Extended Producer Responsibility (EPR) guidelines.

### Judicial Precedents & Central Directives
* **National Green Tribunal (NGT) Principal Bench, New Delhi**:
  * *Almitra H. Patel & Anr. v. Union of India & Ors. (O.A. No. 199/2014)*: Nationwide landmark judgment ordering complete ban on open burning of municipal waste and imposition of spot environmental compensation fines.
  * *In re: Compliance of Municipal Solid Waste Management Rules 2016 (O.A. No. 606/2018)*: Direct financial liability on chief secretaries and municipal commissioners for non-remediated legacy dumpsites.
* **Swachh Bharat Mission (Urban) 2.0 (MoHUA)**:
  * Garbage Free Cities (GFC) Star Rating Certification Protocol and Open Defecation Free (ODF / ODF++ / Water+) guidelines.

## 5. Electricity Distribution, Streetlighting & Electrical Safety

Referenced in: [`backend/kb/india_electricity_and_streetlighting.md`](./india_electricity_and_streetlighting.md) and [`backend/kb/tsspdcl_streetlights.md`](./tsspdcl_streetlights.md)

### Union Legislation & Central Regulatory Authority Regulations
1. **The Electricity Act, 2003 (Act No. 36 of 2003 — Union Legislation)**:
   * *Section 53*: Mandatory safety standards and public hazard prevention for all distribution licensees (DISCOMs).
   * *Section 68*: Overhead power lines, minimum clearances from roadways, buildings, and ground.
   * *Section 161*: Mandatory reporting of electrical accidents, investigations, and prosecution for public negligence.
2. **Central Electricity Authority (Measures relating to Safety and Electric Supply) Regulations, 2010**:
   * *Regulation 29*: Periodic inspection, testing, earthing, and insulation standards for electrical installations.
   * *Regulation 36*: Earth leakage protective devices (ELCB / RCD) on public power apparatus.
   * *Regulation 73*: Mandatory vertical and horizontal clearances for low/medium/high-voltage conductors passing along or across any street (5.8m minimum for high-voltage, 5.5m for low-voltage lines).

### Technical Codes & Standards
* **Bureau of Indian Standards — SP 72:2010**: *National Lighting Code of India* (Public roadway lighting classes M1 to M5 based on traffic volume, pedestrian density, and accident risk).
* **Bureau of Indian Standards — SP 30:2023**: *National Electrical Code of India (NEC)* (Earthing standards and outdoor public lighting safety).
* **Street Lighting National Programme (SLNP)**: Energy Efficiency Services Limited (EESL) CCMS (Centralized Control and Monitoring System) specifications for automated municipal LED switching and energy metering.
* **Ministry of Housing & Urban Affairs (MoHUA)**: *Safe City Project Guidelines* (Mandatory 24x7 public illumination on women safety transit corridors).

## 7. Environmental Pollution & Industrial Hazard Regulation

Referenced in: [`backend/kb/india_environmental_pollution.md`](./india_environmental_pollution.md)

### Central Environmental Statutes
1. **The Air (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act, 1981 (Act No. 14 of 1981)**:
   * *Section 19*: Power to declare air pollution control areas.
   * *Section 21*: Mandatory Consent to Establish (CTE) and Consent to Operate (CTO) for industrial plants.
   * *Section 31A*: Power to issue binding directions for unit closure or electricity/water disconnection.
2. **The Environment (Protection) Act, 1986 (Act No. 29 of 1986)**:
   * Framework legislation empowering central and state authorities to set national environmental standards and prosecute non-compliant polluters.
3. **The National Green Tribunal Act, 2010 (Act No. 19 of 2010)**:
   * Statutory enforcement of civil liability, environmental restitution, and the *Polluter Pays Principle*.
4. **Noise Pollution (Regulation and Control) Rules, 2000 (S.O. 123(E))**:
   * Statutory decibel standards for Industrial (75dB), Commercial (65dB), Residential (55dB), and Silence Zones (50dB).
5. **Hazardous and Other Wastes (Management and Transboundary Movement) Rules, 2016**:
   * Regulation of chemical sludge, toxic effluents, and disposal in authorized TSDF facilities.

## 8. District Administration, Magisterial Powers & Disaster Escalation

Referenced in: [`backend/kb/india_district_administration_and_disaster_management.md`](./india_district_administration_and_disaster_management.md) and [`backend/kb/collector_office.md`](./collector_office.md)

### National Disaster & Administrative Statutes
1. **Disaster Management Act, 2005 (Act No. 53 of 2005 — Union Legislation)**:
   * *Section 30*: Powers and functions of the District Disaster Management Authority (DDMA) to inspect, direct line agencies, and mandate disaster prevention measures.
   * *Section 34*: Magisterial powers of the District Collector / District Magistrate to commandeer resources, evacuate populations, and order summary demolition of hazardous structures.
   * *Section 51*: Penal punishment (imprisonment up to 2 years) for obstruction or refusal to comply with DDMA directions.
2. **Code of Criminal Procedure, 1973 (CrPC) / Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, 2023 (BNSS)**:
   * **Section 133 CrPC / Section 152 BNSS 2023**: *Conditional order for removal of public nuisance.* Empowers the District Magistrate, Sub-Divisional Magistrate (SDM), or Executive Magistrate to pass summary binding orders for the immediate removal of any unlawful obstruction, dangerous building, or toxic emission from public roads or waterways.
3. **Public Liability Insurance Act, 1991 (Act No. 6 of 1991)**:
   * Mandatory compensation and relief for victims of hazardous industrial accidents.
4. **State Land Encroachment & Water Bodies Protection Acts**:
   * Statutory powers vested in District Collectors to remove unauthorized encroachments from lake beds, floodplains, and government revenue lands.

## 9. Public Grievance Redressal Portals & Central Helplines

The SLA escalation benchmarks, automated notices, and citizen tracking channels align with real-world Indian public grievance systems:

* **CPGRAMS (Centralized Public Grievance Redress and Monitoring System)**:
  * Department of Administrative Reforms & Public Grievances (DARPG), Government of India ([pgportal.gov.in](https://pgportal.gov.in)).
* **Swachhata App Platform**:
  * Ministry of Housing & Urban Affairs (MoHUA) national civic grievance portal for urban local bodies.
* **National Emergency Response Support System (ERSS)**:
  * Universal Pan-India Emergency Helpline: **112** (Ministry of Home Affairs).
* **Pan-India Power Outage & Streetlight Emergency**:
  * Universal DISCOM Helpline: **1912** (Ministry of Power).
* **National Water & Sewerage Grievance Helpline**:
  * Universal Jal Helpline: **1916** (Active across major metropolitan boards).
* **Central Pollution Control Board SAMEER Platform**:
  * National Air Quality Index (AQI) and public environmental complaint application.
